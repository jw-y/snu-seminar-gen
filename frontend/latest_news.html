SNU Data Science Seminar “Teaching Machines to Understand the Physical World” Time : 09:00 - 10:30, Sep 2, 2026 Place : Building 43-2, Room B101 (Registration required for in-person attendance) Zoom: https://snu-ac-kr.zoom.us/j/2507466555 Abstract When I started my career about 20 years ago. the term “data science” was not common. We were trying to teach computers how to interpret the world Since then, I have participated in major technology transitions—from computer vision, computational photography, and large-scale visual search to autonomous driving, generative AI, and now physical AI. Along the way, I worked on problems ranging from reconstructing 3D scenes and estimating photo locations to enabling vehicles to perceive their surroundings and building AI systems that learn from billions of real-world observations. Despite their differences, these transitions have revolved around one fundamental question: How do we teach machines to understand the physical world? Rather than focusing on algorithms, this talk shares lessons from building real products and organizations around data. Why is the data pipeline often more important than the model itself? What changes when AI moves beyond the digital domain into cars and robots? I will also explore how “data” has evolved—from pixels to the collective memory of intelligent machines—and why continuous learning from the real world will be essential to physical AI. This is a personal reflection on 25 years of technological change and the opportunities ahead for the next generation of data scientists.
