SNU Data Science Seminar “The Next Human-AI Interface: From Wearable AI to Silent Speech” Time : 09:00 - 10:30, Oct 14, 2026 27001 tp: s/j/2507466555 Abstract AI is moving beyond screens. Smart glasses, earbuds, wristbands, rings, and neural interfaces are bringing AI closer to the body, supporting interaction through voice, gestures, muscle activity, and brain signals. This shift raises a basic question: how can human intent be captured reliably and naturally? This talk examines how wearable AI is changing the way people communicate with machines. It follows a progression from voice and gesture control to muscle sensing, silent articulation, and brain-computer interfaces in everyday human-Al interaction. The second part introduces NeuroTalk, a framework for reconstructing a user's voice from scalp EEG recorded during spoken and imagined speech. Because imagined speech has no matching audio recording, the problem involves noisy signals, limited data, and missing ground truth. I will discuss the model, evaluation results, audio examples, and current limitations, then close with the practical and ethical challenges of making wearable and neural interfaces reliable everyday technologies. Young-Eun Lee Young-Eun Lee is a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences at Stanford University School of Medicine, Her work asks how artificial intelligence can translate brain and physiological signals into useful forms of communication and human-computer interaction. She received her Ph.D. in Brain and Cognitive Engineering from Korea University, where she developed NeuroTalk, a generative framework for reconstructing speech from EEG during spoken and imagined speech. She also helped develop OpenBMI, a public EEG benchmark and open-source toolbox for reproducible brain-computer interface research, and has worked on real-time motion-artifact removal and mobile EEG recording outside conventional laboratory. settings.
