SNU Data Science Seminar “AI Investment Trends: Waiting for the Next ChatGPT Moment” Time : 09:00 - 10:30, Sep 16, 2026 Zoom: https://snu-ac-kr.zoom.us/j/2507466555 Abstract Four years ago I gave a talk in this seminar called" Al Investment Trends." It opened with a slide titled "Global AI funding decline continues." ChatGPT launched three weeks later. Jeff Herbst and I founded GFT Ventures to back the early-stage opportunities we expected to emerge as Al and data science matured. This talk is an argument about where those opportunities actually sit today — and it begins by admitting how thoroughly the last four years rearranged them. Since that morning, AT has gone from roughly half of global venture capital to more than two-thirds. In the first half of 2026 alone, $510 billion was invested worldwide, and two private companies took 43 percent of it. I will walk down the stack from an investor's seat and ask the same question at each layer: where did the money actually end up’ The answers are not the ones the 2022 consensus predicted. The largest startup exit in history went not to a model ‘builder, but to an application built on someone else's models. Then I want to tum to the question I think this room should care about most: Is physical AI about to have its ChatGPT moment? The analogy is tempting — robotics and embodied AI absorbed $47.4 billion in six months. I think it is also misleading, and the reason is data. Language models trained on a corpus that already existed and was free to take. Robots have to manufacture their own data, one teleoperated hour at a time. There is no internet for robots. The one comparable category that has finished the joumey — autonomous driving — took seventeen years and tens of billions of dollars, and left three survivors, each standing on a trillion-dollar balance sheet. No independent startup made it to the end. 1 will close with a forecast in two parts, and with the problems I would want to work on if I were starting out in data science today. Jay Eum Jay Eum is cofounder and Managing Partner of GFT Ventures, an early-stage venture firm investing in AI and physical AT companies at Seed and Series A in North America, He holds an MBA from Stanford University and both a BS and an MS in Chemistry from Seoul National University. He has spent more than 25 years in institutional and corporate venture capital. He co-founded TransLink Capital, where he was a General Partner across four funds with more than $800 million under management and over thirty Asian corporate limited partners. Earlier he launched and led Samsung Ventures America, and began his venture career at Vertex Management, the Silicon Valley arm of Temasek Holdings. He serves as Chairman of the Asia America Multi Technology Association. GFT's portfolio includes Higgsfield AI, Go.ai, and Anyware Robotics.
